# Resistance Workout Reflection

**Age:** 26 | **Height:** 5'11" | **Weight:** 194 lbs | **Training background:** ~3 years of strength training, mostly powerlifting and bodybuilding style splits

## Before the workout

Going in, I was not worried about the strength part of this circuit. I have lifted for about three years, so squats, lunges, push-ups, shoulder press, and curls are all movements I know well. What I was worried about was the pace. Circuits and anything endurance-based are my weak spot. My cardio is honestly poor. I get out of breath on a light incline walk, so I knew three rounds back to back would be a different kind of hard for me than a heavy set of five.

I still watched every video before starting. A couple of them were useful reminders. The reverse lunge video reminded me to keep my front knee tracking over my foot instead of collapsing in, and the push-up video was a good reminder to keep my hips from sagging once I got tired.

## Setup

I used a pair of 15 lb dumbbells for the weighted exercises, a yoga mat, a water bottle, and the timer on my phone. Workout clothes and running shoes.

## Warm-up

I did a 7 minute brisk walk. Even that got my heart rate up more than I would like to admit. By the end I was breathing a little heavy, which told me right away that I needed to pace the circuit and not just attack it.

## The circuit

### Round 1

- **Squats:** 15 reps holding both dumbbells. Easy on the legs. Form felt solid.
- **Reverse lunges:** 10 each leg with dumbbells. Fine, but my balance was a little shaky on the left side.
- **Push-ups:** 15 standard. No issue here.
- **Crunches:** 30 seconds. Manageable. I have only recently started training my core directly, so I could feel it more than I expected.
- **Shoulder press:** 15 reps with the 15s. Fine.
- **Biceps curls:** 15 reps. Fine.
- **Mountain climbers:** 30 seconds. This is where it hit me. About 15 seconds in I was gassed and my pace dropped a lot.
- **Bicycle crunches:** 30 seconds. My abs were burning and my breathing was heavy.

I took about 90 seconds between rounds, mostly to catch my breath. My legs and arms were fine. My lungs were not.

### Round 2

Strength exercises were still comfortable, but I dropped squats and push-ups to 12 reps so I could keep moving. Reverse lunges started to feel more like cardio than a leg exercise. Mountain climbers were rough again. I had to pause twice inside the 30 seconds. Bicycle crunches were slower but I kept going for the full time.

Rest between rounds was closer to two and a half minutes. I was noticeably more winded.

### Round 3

I kept the weights for squats but did the lunges with no weights so I could focus on form and breathing. Push-ups dropped to 10. Crunches and bicycles were the same, just slower. Mountain climbers were the low point. I did them for the full 30 seconds but at maybe half the speed of round one and had to stop once. By the end I was sweating a lot and my heart rate was way up for a workout that never felt heavy.

## Stretching

I followed the 20 minute flexibility routine. I usually skip stretching after lifting, so this was the part I was most tempted to cut. I am glad I did not. My hips and hamstrings were tight from the lunges and the stretch helped bring my heart rate back down slowly instead of just stopping. The comment in the assignment is right. I did not regret it.

## What I learned

**My strength is ahead of my conditioning by a lot.** None of the individual exercises were hard. Fifteen squats or fifteen push-ups on their own are a warm-up for me. But stringing eight exercises together with no rest exposed how weak my cardio base is. The mountain climbers were the clearest example. My legs and shoulders could do them. My lungs could not keep up.

**Core work is newer to me than I like to admit.** I have spent three years building the "show" muscles and chasing numbers on squat, bench, and deadlift. I only recently started paying attention to lower back, abs, and forearms. The crunches and bicycle crunches were humbling. They should not have been that hard for someone who can squat decent weight.

**Pacing matters.** In round one I went at full speed because everything felt easy. That cost me in rounds two and three. Next time I want to start at a pace I can hold for all three rounds.

## Plan going forward

This workout confirmed what I already knew. I am below average for my age group on endurance and I want to fix that. My plan:

1. Add 2 to 3 short cardio sessions per week. Nothing fancy, just 20 to 30 minutes of incline walking or easy cycling, and build from there.
2. Do this circuit or something like it once a week and track rest time between rounds. My goal is to get the rest down to 60 seconds or less within a month.
3. Keep the core and functional work in my routine instead of treating it as an afterthought.
4. Keep stretching after workouts, since I clearly need the mobility work too.

Overall this was a good reality check. It was not hard in the way I am used to. It was hard in the way I need to get better at.
